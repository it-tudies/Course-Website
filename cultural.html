Māori Culture and Values

Māori culture is deeply rooted in the spiritual beliefs and practices of New Zealand’s indigenous people. The revitalization of Māori language and customs is underway in modern society, as the next generation takes on the responsibility of preserving their cultural heritage. Māori values are centered around land, water, and air as essential ingredients of life that are to be respected, cherished, and sustained.

Māori culture is built on a deep connection to land, ancestors, and community, which are essential elements to understanding the traditions that make it so unique. The Māori people have a rich and complex history that spans over 1,000 years. Their origins can be traced back to Polynesian ancestors who migrated from eastern Polynesia around the 13th century. These skilled navigators and seafarers traveled vast distances in double-hulled canoes, braving treacherous seas and unknown lands. They brought with them their language, culture, and traditions, which would eventually shape the unique identity of the Māori people.

Key Māori Culture and Values

Manaakitanga

"Hospitality and Care"

In Māori culture, manaakitanga is a core value that strengthens social bonds and community well-being. It is closely linked to mana, a person’s prestige, authority, and spiritual power, which can be enhanced through acts of care and respect. Practicing manaakitanga is not just about politeness; it is a way to honour and uplift others, whether guests, family, or strangers, while also reinforcing the giver’s own mana.

Kaitiakitanga

"Guardianship of the Environment"

Kaitiakitanga is a Māori concept that signifies guardianship and protection of the environment. It emphasizes the interconnectedness of humans and nature, where all life is created from Papatuanuku (Mother Earth). Kaitiakitanga involves a holistic approach to managing resources, ensuring their sustainability for future generations, and is guided by Māori customs and protocols (tikanga). It is not merely about physical resources but also encompasses cultural, spiritual, and social aspects of the environment, reflecting a deep connection to the land and its resources.

Whanaungatanga

"Relationships"

Whanaungatanga is a core Māori value describing how people form and maintain meaningful relationships. It is grounded in whakapapa (genealogy) but also extends to friends, neighbours, colleagues, and communities. Key aspects include:

  • Belonging and connection: A sense of being part of a collective through shared experiences.
  • Kinship obligations: Responsibilities that strengthen each member of the group.
  • Reciprocity and support: Giving and receiving help in ways that uphold mutual care.
  • Community wellbeing: Strong relationships contribute to resilient, healthy communities.

Rangatiratanga

"Leadership"

Rangatiratanga is a multifaceted concept that combines traditional chiefly authority with modern notions of Māori sovereignty, self-management, and community leadership. It embodies both the right to lead and the responsibility to serve, ensuring the well-being of people, land, and resources for current and future generations.
